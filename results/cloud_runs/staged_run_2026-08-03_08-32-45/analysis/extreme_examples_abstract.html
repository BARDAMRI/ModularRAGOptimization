Extreme Cases: Why G2 Dominates G1 at the Query Level

Deep dive — 10 queries with largest G2−G1 gap

We identify the 10 queries where the centroid-based retrieval group (G2) most dramatically exceeds the query-centered group (G1). Two distinct mechanisms explain the gap. In both, the root cause is the same: queries occupy a sparse region of embedding space, while documents cluster in a structurally denser zone that the centroid of LLM-scored documents (G3) consistently reaches.

Two Mechanisms

Mechanism A — 3 queries (118, 29, 85)
The G3 centroid is pulled away from GT by false-positive documents, so r2 ≥ r1. Yet G2 is large because the centroid lands inside a structurally dense zone of document space. Proximity to GT is not required — presence in document space is sufficient.
Query 118: r2 = 2.1 × r1, G2/G1 = 68×
Mechanism B — 7 queries
G3 forms a coherent, tight cluster. Its centroid falls close to GT (r2 < r1). Even a smaller centroid circle lands inside the dense document cluster around GT and captures many more documents than G1.
Median G3 spread std: 0.088 (vs 0.726 in Mech A)

Conclusions

  1. G1 collapses to 0-1 documents in 9 of 10 extreme cases. Queries sit in a structurally sparse region of embedding space; almost nothing is near the query even when the GT is nearby.
  2. The centroid does not need to be close to GT. Query 118 has r2 more than twice r1, yet G2 = 68. What matters is that the centroid lands in document space, which is inherently denser than query space.
  3. Two paths to a large G2. Mechanism A: centroid displaced by false positives, lands in dense document region. Mechanism B: tight G3 cluster, centroid genuinely close to GT. Both produce the same result: G2 >> G1.
  4. RAG failure does not prevent a large G2. Queries 190 and 153 (RAG failed, GT not in G3) still reach G2 = 56 and 24. G2 reflects the coherent document cluster, not the correctness of scoring.
  5. The density asymmetry between query space and document space is the fundamental cause of the G2 > G1 phenomenon across all 196 queries, and it is most visible at the extremes studied here.
