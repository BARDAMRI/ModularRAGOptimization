Our retriever substantially outperforms published BM25 baselines (67–69%) and is competitive with fine-tuned dense retrieval approaches on the same benchmark.

Why Our Precision Is Higher — Setup Differences

Most published systems search over the full PubMed corpus (~30M+ abstracts) with BM25 or general-purpose embeddings. Our setup uses: - A curated corpus built from PubMedQA's own document pool (~200K documents) - Cosine similarity over dense embeddings (domain-general sentence transformer) - A smaller, task-aligned search space

This explains the gap: searching a task-aligned corpus with dense embeddings is inherently easier than open-domain retrieval over all of PubMed.

References: - Benchmarking RAG for Medicine: https://arxiv.org/html/2402.13178v1 - ModernBERT + ColBERT for biomedical RAG: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.04757 - MRAG Benchmark: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.16503

2. Overlap Between Top-K Docs for GT vs Top-K Docs for Query

For each query we built two sub-pools: - GT-pool: top-K documents closest to the GT embedding - Query-pool: top-K documents closest to the query embedding

The Jaccard overlap between these two pools per query:

K Mean Jaccard Median Jaccard Min Max
5 0.298 0.250 0.000 0.667
10 0.354 0.333 0.000 0.818
20 0.400 0.379 0.026 0.905
50 0.329 0.333 0.010 0.724
100 0.242 0.235 0.010 0.575

Interpretation: At K=5, the two pools share on average only ~30% of their documents. This means the documents most similar to the query embedding are largely different from the documents most similar to the GT embedding. The GT and the query "live" in related but distinct regions of the embedding space — which explains why the retriever (which searches using the query embedding) sometimes fails to find the GT.

The overlap improves slightly as K grows (up to K=20), then decreases again as both pools expand into less relevant territory.

3. Is the GT in the Top-5 Closest Documents to the Query?

Direct answer from the retriever results (199 queries):

Case Queries %
GT is top-1 closest to query (retriever succeeded) 186 93.5%
GT is NOT top-1; position 2–5 unknown 13 6.5%

For 186/199 queries, the GT document is the single closest document to the query in the corpus → it is definitively in top-5.

For the 13 failure cases, we do not have full top-5 retriever output stored. However, the Jaccard analysis (above) suggests that in many of these cases the GT embedding is not close to the query embedding at all — making it unlikely to appear in top-5 either.

Conclusion: The GT is in top-5 for at least 93.5% of queries. For the remaining 6.5%, the GT is likely not in top-5, which is precisely why the retriever fails — and why those are the hardest cases for any retrieval-based approach.

4. Scatter Plot — Pearson & Spearman Correlation Results

The scatter plot below shows LLM score (X-axis) vs distance to GT (Y-axis) for a sample of 30,000 non-GT document pairs and all 199 GT documents (red).

Chart location: analysis/charts/base/scatter_score_vs_dist.png analysis/charts/base/scatter_pearson_spearman.png

Key observations from the scatter: - Data appears in discrete vertical bands (10 possible LLM scores on 1–10 integer scale) - GT documents (red, dist=0) are structural outliers that anchor the regression line - Clear downward trend: higher LLM score → lower distance to GT - Within each score band, distance variance is large — explaining the weak per-query correlation

Correlation summary:

Method Median r Direction Significant (p<0.05)
Spearman −0.166 Negative ✓ 99.5% of queries
Pearson −0.391 Negative ✓ 99.5% of queries
Kendall τ-b −0.134 Negative ✓ 99.5% of queries
Point-biserial +0.146 Positive ✓ 99.5% of queries

All correlations are in the expected direction and statistically significant in virtually all queries. Pearson is stronger than Spearman because it is pulled by the GT outlier point (score=1.0, dist=0.0). The honest estimate of correlation strength is Spearman (−0.17): weak but consistent and real.
